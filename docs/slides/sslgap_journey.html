The measuring stick

What we measure — and what each meter can be fooled by

excess kurtosis
E[x⁴]/σ⁴ − 3
4th-moment deviation from a Gaussian (whose value is 3), along the worst direction. Heavy tails / clusters ⇒ large.
sliced-Gaussianity
normality of random 1-D slices
Foolable: in high dim it only senses mean & covariance, not shape. Never read alone.
moment-KL
½(tr Σ + ‖μ‖² − d − log|Σ|)
Distance of the features' mean & covariance from a unit Gaussian — the calibration meter. ↓ = calibrated.
align_rel
‖a−b‖²pos / ‖a−b‖²rand
View-invariance as a distance ratio (positive vs random pairs). ↓ = invariant.
cos-margin
⟨cos⟩pos − ⟨cos⟩rand
Same idea in cosine: how much closer positive pairs are than random ones. ↑ = invariant.
effective rank
entropy of the singular spectrum
How many dimensions carry variance. Beware: variance-weighted ≠ linear separability.
variance floor
min σ / mean σ (per-dim)
Scale-free anti-collapse check. ≈1 ⇒ no dead dimensions.
class-alignment
kmeans-NMI · between/total var
How much of the geometry lines up with labels — the instrument for semantic structure.
The recurring lesson: these meters dissociate — rank ≠ separability, sliced-Gaussianity ≠ shape. So we never quote one alone and lead isotropy claims with worst-direction stats. align_rel is a distance ratio (easy to misread).

The opening finding

Nothing we measure at h predicts probe quality

At the loss space, the goal owns the geometry perfectly. At the backbone, no marginal statistic tracks how well the features actually probe. So — is there anything that does?
This is the hinge: if no simple statistic ranks the backbone by quality, the ranking question later (can a predictive metric do it?) is worth asking.

Discovery

A goal lives only where the loss imposes it

Each method's goal is enforced at the loss space and fades on the way back to the backbone — the space we actually deploy from.
The goal is sharp where the loss lives and weak at the backbone. (Invariance is margin-scored — backbones are so cone-compact that raw similarity misleads.)

Move 1 · dose from toxic…

Full calibration erases the semantics

At full strength the term is perfectly enforced — and drags the backbone's accuracy back down toward untrained. Our own first guess, killed: over-calibrating flattens the very structure the features need.
We predicted the strong term would keep the structure while calibrating. It did the opposite — accuracy collapses at high dose (the two red bars at the bottom).

Move 1 · …to tonic

A whisper of the same term helps

At low strength it beats the control on every probe — the best backbone in this family we've produced — and it's genuinely enforced (calibration saturates almost immediately). These terms are conditioners with a sweet spot, not destinations.
Same term, opposite verdict at different dose. Low dose is a real improvement and not decorative — calibration is ~90% achieved at the whisper. The payoff of the "balance, not satisfaction" idea.

Move 1 · does it generalize?

The same term on VICReg and DINO

The calibration term makes VICReg better too. For DINO — already the strongest backbone — the improvement is less obvious. (live monitor curves.)
The conditioner looks method-general: it clearly helps VICReg; for DINO, which is already near the top, the gain is marginal. Live curves, not a final verdict.

A sharper question

Can we rank models without labels?

what the model sees
predict →
held-out patch → frozen MAE + RFF

Give each model an image; ask how well its features predict a held-out patch — encoded as a frozen MAE description passed through random Fourier features (RFF). How well it recovers that target is the predictive score, D_read (a held-out linear fit — no labels, no training).

The strongest ranking comes when the target is exactly the frozen MAE tokens of the held-out (non-foveal) region, sketched with RFF — and it's tested on models with the same architecture and data, so a win can't be an artifact.
Methodology: the prediction target is a frozen MAE representation of the held-out patch, passed through random Fourier features; D_read is the held-out linear fit recovering it. Best correlation came with exactly this MAE-tokens+RFF target, on matched architecture/data models.

The result

The predictive score ranks the models — better than anything we measure

Across the whole zoo, the predictive score agrees with true probe accuracy better than every statistic in the battery — and it needs no labels. The one backbone property that tracks quality is how well it predicts what it hasn't seen.
The finale, and the answer to the opening slide: the thing that ranks the backbone by quality is predictive information — label-free, beating every marginal statistic.

✓Nothing we measure at the backbone predicts how well it actually works.

✓A label-free predictive score ranks the models — beating every statistic.

Appendix · instruments

Making the "honesty" checks quantitative

  • A structured-direction count that turns "is this Gaussian?" into a number.
  • Every isotropy check reports three reference points — untrained, covariance-matched, and unit-Gaussian.
  • Detection power is calibrated before any such claim leaves the project.
The battery isn't off-the-shelf — building it correctly is part of the contribution.

Appendix · the ranking metric

How the predictive score is computed

  • Turn the frozen context descriptor into random features (a kernel sketch).
  • D_read = how well a fixed-capacity linear map predicts it from the backbone, held out.
  • Fix the readout capacity for every model — capacity differences were pure noise.
  • Audit the target first — spectrum, concentration, and how label-aligned it is.
Two choices mattered: fixing readout capacity removed a noise source, and auditing the target bounded the "isn't this just distillation?" objection.
