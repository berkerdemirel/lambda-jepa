## PAPER 1 — Zimmermann et al., ICML 2021 (arXiv 2102.08850v4)

### 1a. The theorem and its exact conditions — VERIFIED

**Claim under test** (task formulation; DESIDERATA §3: "Zimmermann: InfoNCE inverts the generative
process **conditional on an assumed latent marginal (uniform on sphere)**"; trd_pi R3(A2) imports
"Zimmermann-/iVAE-class results under theirs"): InfoNCE recovers the true latents up to
affine/orthogonal transformation CONDITIONAL on an assumed latent marginal.

**The generative setup (§3, "Theory").** The assumptions are exactly as our docs state — and they
are assumptions on the *ground-truth* latent distribution of an explicit generative model:

> "we assume that the observations x ∈ X are generated by an invertible (i.e., injective)
> generative process g : Z → X […] Influenced by the commonly used feature normalization in
> InfoNCE, we further assume that Z is the unit hypersphere S^{N−1} (see Appx. A.1.1).
> Additionally, we assume that the ground-truth marginal distribution of the latents of the
> generative process is uniform and that the conditional distribution (under which positive pairs
> have high density) is a von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution:
> p(z) = |Z|^{−1}, p(z|z̃) = C_p^{−1} e^{κ z^T z̃} […] (2)
> Given these assumptions, we will show that if f minimizes the contrastive loss L_contr, then f
> solves the demixing problem, i.e., inverts g up to orthogonal linear transformations." (§3)

**Theorem 1 (§3.1)** — the cross-entropy bridge itself conditions on the uniform marginal (it is a
hypothesis of the theorem, not an afterthought):

> "**Theorem 1** (L_contr converges to the cross-entropy between latent distributions). *If the
> ground-truth marginal distribution p is uniform, then for fixed τ > 0, as the number of negative
> samples M → ∞, the (normalized) contrastive loss converges to*
> lim_{M→∞} L_contr(f; τ, M) − log M + log |Z| = E_{z∼p(z)} [H(p(·|z), q_h(·|z))]  (3)
> *where H is the cross-entropy between the ground-truth conditional distribution p over positive
> pairs and a conditional distribution q_h parameterized by the model f*,
> q_h(z̃|z) = C_h(z)^{−1} e^{h(z̃)^T h(z)/τ}" (§3.1)

**Theorem 2 (§3.2)** — the headline hypersphere identifiability result:

> "**Theorem 2.** *Let Z = S^{N−1}, the ground-truth marginal be uniform, and the conditional a
> vMF distribution (cf. Eq. 2). Let the restriction of the mixing function g to Z be injective and
> h be differentiable in a vicinity of Z. If the assumed form of q_h, as defined above, matches
> that of p, and if f is differentiable and minimizes the CL loss as defined in Eq. (1), then for
> fixed τ > 0 and M → ∞, h = f ◦ g is linear, i.e., f recovers the latent sources up to an
> orthogonal linear transformation and a constant scaling factor.*" (§3.2)

Immediately after: "Note that we do not assume knowledge of the ground-truth generative model g;
we only make assumptions about the conditional and marginal distribution of the latents." (§3.2)

**Theorems 5–6 (§3.3)** — convex bodies, general similarities. Marginal again uniform: "As
earlier, we assume that the ground-truth marginal distribution of the latents is uniform. However,
we now assume that the conditional distribution is exponential: p(z) = |Z|^{−1},
p(z|z̃) = C_p^{−1} e^{−δ(z,z̃)}" (§3.3, Eq. 5), with the loss's similarity replaced by δ
(Definition 1, L_δ-contr, Eq. 6).

> "**Theorem 5.** *Let Z be a convex body in R^N, h = f ◦ g : Z → Z, and δ be a metric or a
> semi-metric (cf. Lemma 1 in Appx. A.2.4), induced by a norm. Further, let the ground-truth
> marginal distribution be uniform and the conditional distribution be as Eq. (5). Let the mixing
> function g be differentiable and injective. If the assumed form of q_h matches that of p, i.e.,
> q_h(z̃|z) = C_q^{−1}(z) e^{−δ(h(z̃),h(z))/τ} […] and if f is differentiable and minimizes the
> L_δ-contr objective in Eq. (6) for M → ∞, we find that h = f ◦ g is invertible and affine, i.e.,
> we recover the latent sources up to affine transformations.*" (§3.3)

> "**Theorem 6.** *Let Z be a convex body in R^N, h : Z → Z, and δ be an L^α metric or semi-metric
> (cf. Lemma 1 in Appx. A.2.4) for α ≥ 1, α ≠ 2. Further, let the ground-truth marginal
> distribution be uniform and the conditional distribution be as Eq. (5), and let the mixing
> function g be differentiable and invertible. If the assumed form of q_h(·|z) matches that of
> p(·|z), i.e., both use the same metric δ up to a constant scaling factor, and if f is
> differentiable and minimizes the L_δ-contr objective in Eq. (6) for M → ∞, we find that
> h = f ◦ g is a composition of input independent permutations, sign flips and rescaling.*" (§3.3)

And the loss↔assumed-conditional coupling is explicit:

> "Note that the model distribution q_h, which is implicitly described by the choice of the
> objective, must be of the same form as the ground-truth distribution p, i.e., both must be based
> on the same metric. Thus, identifying different ground-truth conditional distributions requires
> different contrastive L_δ-contr objectives." (§3.3, after Theorem 5)

**Answer to the task's three sub-questions.**
- *Which distribution:* ground-truth latent **marginal uniform** (on S^{N−1} for Th. 1–2; on a
  convex body in R^N for Th. 5–6) + assumed **conditional** (vMF for Th. 2; exponential in a
  norm-induced (semi-)metric δ for Th. 5–6), with the model form q_h required to match p's form.
- *Which similarity:* dot product on L2-normalized outputs (Eq. 1) for Th. 1–2; the general
  similarity −δ(·,·)/τ in L_δ-contr (Def. 1, Eq. 6) for Th. 5–6 — same δ as the assumed conditional.
- *Identifiability class:* **orthogonal linear transformation + constant scaling** (sphere,
  Th. 2); **invertible affine** (convex body, Th. 5); **generalized permutation** = permutation +
  sign flips + rescaling (L^α, α ≥ 1, α ≠ 2, Th. 6).

**Verdict: VERIFIED.** The conditioning on an assumed latent marginal is not merely present — it
is a stated hypothesis of every identifiability theorem in the paper, including the Theorem-1
bridge. One wording precision for our docs: "uniform on sphere" is exact for Th. 1–2; the
convex-body track (Th. 5–6) assumes uniform on a convex body, not a sphere.

### 1b. "Anti-collapse terms = the assumed latent prior of an implicit generative model, which identifiability results condition on" — VERIFIED-WITH-AMENDMENT

**What licenses the paraphrase (all verbatim):**

1. The uniformity (negative-sample) term is read as entropy maximization of the learned marginal
   from the start: "a commonly used contrastive loss from the InfoNCE family […] asymptotically
   converges to a sum of two losses: an alignment loss that pulls together the representations of
   positive pairs, and a uniformity loss that maximizes the entropy of the learned latent
   distribution." (§1)
2. The loss is explicitly said to encode generative assumptions implicitly: "Our theory reveals
   implicit assumptions encoded in the InfoNCE objective about the generative process underlying
   the data." (§1) And for the conditional: "the model distribution q_h, which is implicitly
   described by the choice of the objective" (§3.3).
3. In the proof of Theorem 1, the cross-entropy-to-the-latent-model decomposition literally
   *recovers* Wang & Isola's two terms: "= L_align(f; τ) + L_uni(f; τ) + log |Z|, (29) which
   recovers the original alignment term and the uniformity term for maximimizing [sic] entropy by
   means of a von Mises-Fisher KDE up to the constant log |Z|." (Appx. A.1.2, Eq. 29)
4. The conclusion names the uniform marginal as the prior implicitly encoded in the loss: "it is
   an interesting open question how to adapt it to marginal distributions beyond **the uniform
   implicitly encoded in InfoNCE**." (§5) Also: "To succeed, these objectives implicitly encode a
   few weak assumptions about the statistical nature of the underlying generative factors." (§5)
5. And, per 1a, every identifiability theorem conditions on that marginal.

Chain: uniformity term = entropy maximizer of the learned marginal → its target (uniform on Z) is
exactly the marginal the theorems assume of the generative process → and that marginal is
"implicitly encoded in InfoNCE." The paraphrase is a fair compression of the paper's own framing.

**Amendments (what the paraphrase should not be stretched to say):**

1. **Terminology/scope:** "anti-collapse" appears nowhere in the paper (grep: 0 hits). Zimmermann
   licenses the equation only for the **InfoNCE uniformity/negative-sample term**. Extending it to
   anti-collapse terms of *other* families (VICReg variance-covariance, BYOL predictor+EMA, …) is
   the LDM paper's move (see 2a), not Zimmermann's.
2. **The paper itself proves a prior-free role for the same term** (directly relevant to whether
   the prior-reading is the *only* reading): Appendix A.5 shows uniformity ⇒ bijectivity for *any*
   regular marginal — "a loss term ensuring that the encoded features are distributed according to
   a regular density function, such as the uniformity term, makes the map h bijective and prevents
   an information loss. **Note that this does not assume that the marginal distribution of the
   ground-truth latents p(z) is uniform but only that it is regular and non-vanishing.**"
   (Appx. A.5, after Corollary 1). With the caveat: "the requirement of h being fully
   differentiable is most likely violated in large unregularized neural networks with ReLU
   nonlinearities. Here, one might need the full contrastive loss to ensure bijectivity of h."
   So within Zimmermann: exact-class identifiability conditions on the assumed prior; bijectivity
   (no information collapse) does not.
3. **The conditioning is theorem-level, not empirical-fragility:** "Mismatches in assumptions on
   the marginal and conditional do not lead to a significant drop in performance with respect to
   affine identifiability, but do for permutation identifiability compared to the empirical upper
   bound." (§4.1) And the breaking point is characterized: "While, we observe significant
   robustness to mismatch, in both cases, we see performance drop drastically once the marginal
   distribution is more concentrated than the conditional distribution of positive pairs."
   (§4.1/Fig. 2 discussion). A doc sentence citing this row should not imply the identifiability
   *empirically collapses* the moment the prior is wrong.

**Verdict: VERIFIED-WITH-AMENDMENT** — fair paraphrase of their setup and of what the theorems
condition on; keep amendments 1–3 attached wherever the row is cited.

## PAPER 2 — Mikulasch & Zenke, "Understanding Self-Supervised Learning via Latent Distribution Matching", arXiv 2605.03517v3, ICML 2026 Spotlight

### 2a. Decomposition + family tree — mappings VERIFIED; "every surveyed" VERIFIED-WITH-AMENDMENT

**The decomposition.** Abstract: "We cast SSL as latent distribution matching (LDM): learning
representations that maximize their log-probability under an assumed latent model (alignment),
while maximizing latent entropy to prevent collapse (uniformity)." Formally (§4, Eq. 5):

> "F_LDM = −D_KL[R(z, z′) ∥ P_θ(z, z′)] = ⟨log P_θ(z, z′)⟩_{R(z,z′)} [Alignment]
> + H_R[z, z′] [Uniformity]" (Eq. 5; the bracketed labels are the paper's own underbraces)

THEORY_MAP's formal-object cell "F_LDM = −D_KL[R(z,z′)∥P_θ(z,z′)]: alignment to an assumed latent
model + entropy" is verbatim-correct. A companion variant F_MI = F_LDM + MI term (Eq. 6, after
Aitchison & Ganev 2024) reduces to "⟨log P_θ(z, z′)⟩_{R(z,z′)} + 2H_R[z]" — still alignment +
entropy, with the joint entropy replaced by twice the single-variable entropy.

**The estimator-generated family tree.** §4.2 states the generator explicitly:

> "Depending on the approximation, it is possible to recover contrastive, non-contrastive, or
> other SSL approaches (for details, see Appendix A.5). Entropy estimation based on kernel density
> estimation (KDE) leads to contrastive SSL such as SimCLR. In contrast, parametric estimation can
> be related to non-contrastive SSL such as VICReg. We further show that conditional entropy
> estimation with a predictor is implicitly implemented by SSL models with a predictor and
> stopgrad (Section 6)." (§4.2)

**Table 1** ("Summary of model mapping to the LDM framework"), transcribed — columns: SSL method /
latent distribution P_θ (prior + cond.) / entropy estimator / MI max. (● = F_MI, ○ = F_LDM):

| Method | P_θ (prior + cond.) | Entropy estimator | goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| VICReg (Bardes et al., 2021) | flat + cond. Gaussian | parametric (Gaussian entropy) | ● |
| SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) | uniform + cond. von Mises-Fisher | kernel density | ● |
| CPC (Oord et al., 2018) | empirical + predictive cond. von Mises-Fisher | see Aitchison and Ganev (2024) | ● |
| BYOL/SimSiam (Grill et al., 2020; Chen and He, 2021) | empirical + cond. von Mises-Fisher | conditional entropy plugin | ○ |
| JEPA (e.g., Bardes et al., 2024; Mohammadi et al., 2025) | predictive cond. Gaussian | conditional entropy plugin | ○ |

**Per-mapping quotes and verdicts:**

- **KDE → contrastive (SimCLR): VERIFIED.** "If the entropy is approximated via KDE with bandwidth
  1/β then based on F_MI we recover the original goal function (Appendix A.6.1) […] Thus, SimCLR
  can be understood as LDM to a conditional vMF distribution with added MI maximization." (§5,
  Eqs. 10–11). Fine print (A.6.1): "the SimCLR goal differs from this goal function in that in
  SimCLR the negative samples x⁻ include x′, the positive sample, while in LDM all samples in the
  entropy are i.i.d.—in practice, however, we did not observe a significant difference."
- **Parametric-Gaussian → VICReg: VERIFIED.** "The goal function of VICReg can be derived from
  F_MI by employing the parametric entropy estimator under a normal distribution (Appendix A.6.1)"
  (§5, Eq. 8); via Shwartz-Ziv et al.'s log-determinant reading and a Taylor expansion "which is
  similar to the regularizer in VICReg. Thus, VICReg can be understood as LDM to a conditional
  normal distribution, with additional MI maximization between latent representations." (§5)
  Note the hedges: "can be related", "approximately", "similar to".
- **Conditional-entropy-with-predictor → BYOL/SimSiam stopgrad: VERIFIED.** The estimator
  (A.5.4, Eq. 35): "the cross entropy to train the predictor is exactly equal to the entropy
  estimator of the conditional entropy. This means that when we train a predictor R̂_θ(z′|z) via
  cross-entropy, we are simultaneously obtaining an entropy estimate for free." The stopgrad
  identity (A.6.2, Eq. 54, "equal in derivative"): "both goal functions match in derivative if the
  second term approximates the (derivative of the) conditional entropy of R(z_t|z_{:t}) […] The
  stopgrad approach can therefore be seen to approximately maximize conditional entropy via z_t."
  BYOL/SimSiam specifically (A.6.2, "BYOL/SimSiam", Eqs. 56–58): "We first apply this idea to
  models in the nontemporal setting. To derive the goal function of BYOL (Grill et al., 2020) and
  SimSiam (Chen et al., 2020) we make the choices P_θ(z) = R(z), P_θ(z′|z) = (1/Z) exp(τ p(z)^T z′)
  […] Here we employ the same trick as in A.6.2 and replace the conditional entropy with the
  stopgrad entropy estimator. This recovers the original goal function […] ∝ ⟨τ p(z)^T SG[z′]⟩."
  Caveats the paper itself attaches: the match is in derivative and approximate; "this requires to
  train P_θ on a faster timescale than the encoders. Some stopgrad based approaches ensure this
  heuristically by employing exponentially moving average encoder targets" (A.6.2); and the
  BYOL/SimSiam prior is *empirical*, P_θ(z) = R(z).
- **CPC mapped: VERIFIED** (with a deferral). Table 1 row above; §6: "For F_MI, single-timestep
  entropy can easily be maximized through either KDE, kNN, or parametric estimators. Note that

- **JEPA mapped: VERIFIED.** Table 1 row; §6: "predictive SSL approaches with stopgrad (Grill et

### 2b. The identifiability theorem, its scope, and the "temporal/predictive" scoping — VERIFIED

**Abstract:** "We further prove that predictive LDM yields identifiable latent representations
under mild assumptions, even with nonlinear predictors." — the "even with nonlinear predictors"
phrase in THEORY_MAP §6.3 is the paper's own wording.

**Main-text theorem (§7, "Identifiability in predictive models"):**

> "We consider predictive models that factorize the joint latent distribution as
> P_θ(z) = ∏_t P_θ(z_t|z_{:t}), and assume that learning proceeds by matching this distribution
> and the empirical latent distribution R(z) induced by encoder f.
>
> **Theorem 1** (Identifiability of predictive distribution matching under a Gaussian predictor).
> *Assume that: (i) the model and true predictive distributions are Gaussian of the form:
> P(z_t|z_{:t}) = N(z_t; p(z_{:t}), Σ), with (potentially nonlinear) prediction function p(·) and
> non-degenerate covariance Σ; (ii) the encoder is invertible on the data manifold; (iii) the
> predictor covers the latent space. Then, at the optimum of the LDM objective, the learned
> representation recovers the true latent variables up to an affine transformation.*" (§7)

Follow-up in the same section: "Identifiability arises from the local noise structure of the
prediction errors: the assumed Gaussian form of the predictive residuals constrains admissible
transformations of the latent space, ruling out arbitrary nonlinear reparameterizations (Fig. 5A).
Importantly, the result does not require strong assumptions on the expressiveness or architecture
of the predictor itself beyond coverage of the latent space." And: "**Theorem 2.** *Under
assumptions (ii) and (iii) and assuming a von Mises-Fisher predictive model the LDM objective
recovers the true latent space up to an affine transformation.*" (§7)

**Formal restatement (Appx. A.7.1)** — the scope conditions in full:

> "**Definition 1** (Data generation process). We start by defining the true latent variable
> distribution P(z̃) = ∏_t P(z̃_t|z̃_{:t}), P(z̃_t|z̃_{:t}) = N(z̃_t; p(z̃_{:t}), Σ). […] We assume
> an injective data generation process g […]
> **Theorem 1 (restated).** *(i) (Data generation process) Data is generated by the predictive
> latent process Eq. (61) with invertible covariance Σ and a differentiable and injective data
> generation function g : R^n → R^m. (ii) (Distribution matching) The model is defined through a
> sufficiently flexible encoder f : R^m → R^n to achieve perfect LDM (i.e., reach form 62), and f
> is differentiable and invertible on the image of g, that is, h = f ◦ g is invertible.
> (iii) (Predictor covers the latent space) The Jacobian J_{p_R}(h(z_{:t})) has full row rank,
> equal to the latent dimension n (and so does J_p(z_{:t})). Then, distribution matching recovers
> the true latent variables up to affine transformations, that is, if the form of R matches the
> form of P, the transforming function between latent variables is affine h(z_t) = Az_t + c.*"
> (A.7.1; z_{:t} = {z_s : 0 ≤ s < t} per §6, Eq. 12)

(trd_pi R3(A2) writes the conclusion as f(x) = A s★ + b; the paper's form h(z_t) = Az_t + c with
h = f ∘ g is the same statement composed with g.)

**Is "scoped to predictive SSL on temporal data" accurate? Yes.**
- The theorems live in §7 "Identifiability in **predictive** models" and are stated over the
  predictive factorization ∏_t P_θ(z_t|z_{:t}); the framework generalization that introduces this
  factorization is explicitly temporal: "An appealing feature of the LDM framework is that it also
  allows us to treat **temporal models**. To this end, we first generalize the goal function to
  the temporal case" (§6, Eq. 12), where "z_{:t} = {z_s : 0 ≤ s < t}".
- Definition 1's true data-generating process is a time-indexed latent process; all empirical
  validation (§6.1, §7.1) is on dynamical systems/timeseries (Kalman/RNN predictors, synthetic
  videos, rat hippocampal recordings); the abstract's claim sentence names "predictive LDM".
- Conversely, for the **pairwise/augmentation** case the paper does not state its own
  identifiability theorem — it cites the prior line: "this conditional LDM has been shown to
  recover the underlying latent variables up to trivial transformations when the data-generating
  process is invertible (Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Zimmermann et al., 2021; Roeder et al., 2021).
  We will use this property for proving the theorems in Section 7." (§4)
- **One precision note:** the theorem statements themselves never use the word "temporal"; the
  binding scope is the *predictive factorization over the sequence index t* (a two-element
  sequence — i.e., a pair with a predictor — is a degenerate case the paper neither claims nor
  excludes; its BYOL/SimSiam derivation is explicitly "in the nontemporal setting" but is a
  goal-function derivation, not an identifiability theorem). "Scoped to predictive SSL on temporal
  data" matches how the paper states, proves, and validates the result; if maximal precision is
  wanted, "scoped to predictive models P_θ(z)=∏_t P_θ(z_t|z_{:t}) (temporal in all of the paper's
  statements and experiments)" is the airtight form.

Boundary conditions the paper itself reports (relevant to importing A2 into trd_pi R3):
