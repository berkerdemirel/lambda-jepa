Loss Space ≠ Representation Space

Do modern SSL methods impose their stated desiderata — alignment, invariance, variance/covariance control, isotropy, non-collapse, redundancy reduction, predictive sufficiency — on the backbone representation used downstream, or on a nonlinear projector/predictor head that is thrown away? What breaks in the gap, and how do we measure it?

Executive summary

Answer to the headline question: mostly no — the stated desiderata are enforced in the head's output space z, not on the backbone representation h that everyone actually uses. Every major joint-embedding method (SimCLR, MoCo v2/v3, BYOL, SwAV, DINO, iBOT, DINOv2, Barlow Twins, VICReg) applies its loss after a nonlinear MLP projector — and sometimes an additional predictor or a 65k–131k-way prototype layer — then discards that head and evaluates the backbone. MAE applies its loss in pixel space after a full decoder; I-JEPA applies it in the latent space of a separate predictor against an EMA teacher. Even LeJEPA (2025) — which strips away the field's other heuristics (no stop-gradient, no EMA teacher, no schedulers) and proves an isotropy-optimality theorem — still applies both its predictive loss and its SIGReg regularizer to the output of a 3-layer MLP projector (512→2048→2048→proj, BatchNorm), then probes the backbone. Across all eleven methods surveyed, none impose their loss on the representation actually used downstream.

This is not an accident of engineering but a load-bearing design: the head is where invariance, uniformity, whitening, and collapse-pressure are absorbed so that the backbone doesn't have to satisfy them. The gap is real, measured, and large — and it means most SSL theory certifies a space that is thrown away.

Six established findings

  1. EThe loss space is the worse representation. SimCLR's own ablation: the pre-projector layer h beats the post-projector z by >10 points of linear-probe accuracy; Guillotine Regularization (Bordes et al., TMLR 2023) shows the trunk-vs-projector probe gap reaches ~30 points across SSL methods, while supervised networks show the opposite layer profile.
  2. EInvariance is realized at z, not h. SimCLR Table 3: rotation is predictable from h at 67.6% but from z at 25.6%. RCDM diffusion visualizations (Bordes et al., TMLR 2022) "debunk an often restated but mistaken belief": SSL backbone representations are not invariant to their training augmentations — post-projector embeddings are.
  3. EWhitening/variance/redundancy constraints are satisfied exactly at z and only directionally at h. VICReg's variance target is met exactly on the 8192-d expander output and merely "translates in an increased standard deviation at the representation level"; VICReg contains no ablation regularizing h directly. Barlow Twins' decorrelation-to-identity is computed on the projector output and improves monotonically with projector width while the probed backbone stays fixed at 2048-d.
  4. ECollapse-avoidance is implemented by head-side machinery. BYOL without its predictor collapses to 0.3%; SimSiam without it hits 0.1%; DINO's centering+sharpening act on prototype logits; DirectPred replaces the whole predictor with a closed-form spectral statistic; the rank-differential account (Zhuo et al., ICLR 2023) locates anti-collapse in a rank difference between the two branch outputs.
  5. EAlmost all guarantees bind z. Alignment/uniformity (Wang & Isola), the Arora contrastive bound, HaoChen's spectral-contrastive linear-probe guarantee, the Laplacian/MDS equivalences (Balestriero & LeCun), VICReg's information-theoretic bounds — all are statements about the layer the loss touches. Saunshi et al. (ICML 2022) prove that loss-value-only analyses can be vacuous, which is exactly the degree of freedom the head exploits.
  6. EThe field is converging on closing the gap from both ends. Backbone-side guarantees now exist (Wen & Li 2022 for the encoder under a predictor; U-MAE 2022 pulling MAE's pixel loss back to encoder-level alignment; Ouyang et al., ICLR 2025: "projection head is secretly an information bottleneck" with pre-projector guarantees), and two 2025–26 works reframe the problem: LeJEPA (which proves the isotropic Gaussian is optimal for worst-case downstream risk — though, as its implementation confirms, it enforces that distribution on an MLP projector and still probes the backbone) and Latent Distribution Matching (Mikulasch & Zenke, ICML 2026 Spotlight: SSL = alignment to an assumed latent model + entropy maximization, with an identifiability theorem).

What follows from the gap

PThree consequences organize the report. (i) Metric mislocation: alignment, uniformity, isotropy, effective rank, and redundancy have been mostly measured — when measured at all — in the space where they were optimized; measuring the same quantities at h is cheap, largely undone, and is the core of the proposed study (§3, §5). (ii) Semantic failure modes are invisible to the loss: object-absent crops, background shortcuts, feature suppression, and masked-region ambiguity all satisfy the loss in z-space while corrupting what h encodes (§4). (iii) Evaluation-protocol fragility: family rankings (contrastive vs MIM vs JEPA) move by 5–17 points depending on probe choice (MAE's linear-vs-finetune decorrelation; V-JEPA's 16-point attentive-probe dependence), so any audit must fix probes per space, not per method (§5, §7).

The one-sentence research opportunity

ONo published work systematically measures each method's own stated desideratum in both spaces (h and z) across the modern method zoo under matched backbones and data — the closest antecedents (Guillotine Regularization; Dubois et al. risk decomposition) measure accuracy, not desiderata. A "metrics × models × spaces" audit (§3) plus failure-oriented probes (§5) is a well-scoped, compute-feasible first paper with a falsifiable central claim: SSL methods satisfy their advertised properties in the space they discard, and the properties that survive into the backbone are different ones — sufficiency-like, not minimality-like.

2 · The MLP/projector gap — what the head absorbs

This section reviews everything found on the role of projectors and predictors beyond the original method papers: ablations, mechanistic studies, and theory. The evidence supports a specific claim: the head is a sacrificial buffer that absorbs the pretext task's constraints — invariance, uniformity, whitening, low-rank pressure — so the backbone doesn't have to satisfy them. Five partially-overlapping explanations exist; none unifies all the evidence.

2.1 The phenomenon, quantified

  • EGuillotine Regularization (Bordes, Balestriero, Garrido, Bardes, Vincent — TMLR 2023) names and measures the trick: removing the head is "critical for SSL methods to display competitive performances on ImageNet for which more than 30 percentage points can be gained." Their framing is this report's thesis verbatim: "one would hope that the network layer at which invariance is explicitly enforced by the SSL criterion during training (the last projector layer) should be the one to use for best generalization performance downstream. But it seems not to be." Supervised networks show the opposite layer profile. Crucially, the optimal readout layer is task-dependent: ImageNet probes peak at the trunk, but "the best performances for Eurosat, CLEVR and Imagenet 1% are obtained at the first projector layer." And the gap is causally tied to pretext/downstream misalignment: with class-defined positive pairs (perfectly aligned pretext), the projector level beats the backbone.
  • ERCDM (Bordes, Balestriero, Vincent — TMLR 2022) settles where invariance lives by generating images conditioned on each space: "SSL (backbone) representation are not invariant to the data augmentations they were trained with — thus debunking an often restated but mistaken belief; SSL post-projector embeddings appear indeed invariant to these data augmentation." Backbone features demonstrably retain scale, position, and color-style.
  • ENot SSL-specific: t-ReX (Sariyildiz et al., ICLR 2023 spotlight) adds an expendable projector to supervised training and gains transfer SOTA, showing "the design of the projector allows us to control the trade-off between performance on the training task and transferability." The buffer effect is objective-agnostic.

2.2 Rank and dimensional collapse: the head as spectral shield

  • EJing et al. (ICLR 2022): contrastive embeddings dimensionally collapse ("span a lower-dimensional subspace") via strong augmentation + implicit low-rank regularization; a linear projector "only needs to be low-rank" (Prop. 2); and — the shield claim — "Without a projector, SimCLR suffers from dimensional collapse in the representation space." DirectCLR (loss on a sub-vector of h, no trainable projector) beats SimCLR-with-linear-projector (62.7 vs 61.1) but not the nonlinear head (66.5).
  • ERank Differential Mechanism (Zhuo et al., ICLR 2023): every asymmetric device (predictor, EMA, SwAV's Sinkhorn, DINO's sharpening) is a spectral filter creating a consistent rank difference between branch outputs, provably raising effective dimensionality. Explicitly does not connect output-rank to backbone rank — a named open edge.
  • ERankMe (Garrido et al., ICML 2023) measures smooth effective rank of embeddings z and uses it for label-free model selection — it works because "performance on embeddings and representations scales almost monotonically." The h↔z rank monotonicity is an empirical regularity, not a theorem. α-ReQ (Agrawal et al., NeurIPS 2022) is its backbone-side complement: eigenspectrum decay coefficient α of backbone covariance predicts linear-readout quality. No paper bridges the two spaces formally. O
  • ESmall projectors are possible (Agrawal et al., NeurIPS 2024): with a stronger orthogonalization constraint, reduced projector dimensionality suffices — evidence the giant-head requirement is an optimization artifact, not fundamental.

2.3 The predictor as closed-form spectral machinery

  • EDirectPred (Tian, Chen, Ganguli, ICML 2021): the linear predictor's eigenspace provably aligns with its input correlation; replacing the trained predictor with a closed-form spectral function matches BYOL (72.4 vs 72.5). Removing stop-gradient provably collapses (Thm 2).
  • EWen & Li (NeurIPS 2022) — the gap-aware exception: their theorem is about the encoder beneath the head. The trainable head produces a substitution effect and an acceleration effect that jointly make the encoder "learn all the features rather than focus only on learning the stronger features." The head exists so the backbone learns balanced features.
  • ESimSiam (Chen & He, CVPR 2021): predictor removal → 0.1%; the paper's own hypothesis is that the optimal predictor approximates the augmentation-conditional expectation ET[f(T(x))] — i.e. the predictor computes the marginalization the loss pretends the backbone performs. Zhang et al. (ICLR 2022) later refuted parts of SimSiam's self-explanation via gradient decomposition; Richemond et al. showed BYOL works without batch statistics (73.9 vs 74.3), killing the BN-as-negatives folk theory — the field's understanding of its own anti-collapse machinery has been empirically wrong at least twice. P

2.4 The head absorbs augmentation information — and can be redirected

  • ESimCLR Table 3 (§1) is the original measurement; RCDM the visual proof.
  • EAugSelf (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2021): adding augmentation-parameter prediction preserves augmentation-aware information in h and improves transfer — implying default training leaves less of it than optimal.
  • ELooC (Xiao et al., ICLR 2021): route invariances into separate head spaces ("each of which is invariant to all but one augmentation"), keeping the shared backbone richer.
  • ECASSLE (Przewięźlikowski et al., KBS 2024): condition the projector on the augmentation parameters so invariance concentrates in the head by construction; their probes show vanilla models lose augmentation-awareness progressively through extractor and projector.
  • PTogether these are a design lesson: the invariance/information split between h and z is steerable, which makes it a measurable, controllable variable for the proposed study rather than a fixed fact of SSL.

2.5 Theories of why the head helps — five families, no unification

  1. Buffer / misalignment: the head absorbs pretext-specific constraints; gap shrinks as pretext↔downstream alignment rises. (Guillotine; SimCLR's conjecture; RCDM; VICReg's expander rationale; t-ReX.) The most direct measurement: Ma et al. (IJCAI 2024) show the projector is "a uniform projector, regardless of whether the uniformity part appears explicitly in the objective function" — even SimSiam, whose loss has no uniformity term — and report backbone entropy consistently below projector entropy (SimCLR 1.75 vs 1.85; MoCo-v2 1.51 vs 2.03; CIFAR-10/ResNet-18), reading the encoder as offloading the uniformity burden onto the head. E (Note: a 2026 preprint framing the head as a "universal geometric buffer" with implicit-whitening claims, arXiv:2605.17180, failed independent adversarial verification in this study — see §7 — and is deliberately not relied upon here.)
  2. Rank protection: the head absorbs the objective's low-rank pressure, keeping h high-rank (Jing; RDM; Dubois et al.: heads "improve effective dimensionality and thus usability").
  3. Implicit bias / feature weighting: Xue et al. (ICLR 2024): training progressively unequalizes feature weights with depth; "lower layers tend to have more normalized and less specialized representations," and nonlinear heads let lower layers keep features "completely absent in higher layers" — also explains robustness gains, and transfers to supervised learning.
  4. Information bottleneck: Ouyang et al. (ICLR 2025): downstream guarantees for pre-projector features; "an effective projector should act as an information bottleneck, filtering out the information irrelevant to the contrastive objective." Ma et al. (IJCAI 2024): the head implements the uniformity term specifically.
  5. Dynamics / asymmetry (predictor-specific): eigenspace alignment, variance asymmetry between branches, closed-form replaceability (DirectPred; Wang et al. CVPR 2022; Halvagal et al. NeurIPS 2023; Richemond et al. 2023 — optimal linear predictor ≈ orthogonal projection, EMA+stop-grad as "an efficient orthonormalization mechanism").

PFamilies 1 and 4 are informationally near-equivalent (absorbing a constraint = filtering the information that violates it); 2 and 5 are linked through spectral filtering. A unification would need to explain the one result none of them predicts cleanly: Dubois et al.'s finding that nonlinear heads add no effective-dimensionality benefit over linear ones ("we still do not completely understand the impact of non-linear projections"). 2.6 What remains genuinely open

  • OA quantitative transfer law. Nobody has measured how much of any z-space property (invariance level, decorrelation, uniformity, rank) survives at h as a function of head depth/width/nonlinearity. All existing evidence is qualitative (VICReg D.8, RCDM, CASSLE probes).
  • OPrincipled readout-layer selection. Guillotine shows the optimal layer varies by task; no a-priori criterion exists. RankMe/α-ReQ rank whole models, not layers.
  • OPrototype-head anatomy at scale. No dedicated analysis of what DINOv2/iBOT's 65k–131k prototype layers absorb, nor guillotine curves for masked-token heads.
  • OThe nonlinearity mystery. Dubois et al.: nonlinear heads don't add effective dimensionality over linear ones, yet SimCLR's +3% nonlinear-over-linear is robust. Xue et al. explain part of it in stylized models only.
  • OHead-free at SOTA scale. DirectPred/DirectCLR replace or shrink the head only in (near-)linear regimes and trail full nonlinear heads slightly (62.7 vs 66.5; 72.4 vs 72.5). No surveyed method — including LeJEPA, which keeps a wide 3-layer MLP projector — trains head-free at scale without loss. Whether the nonlinear projector is eliminable at SOTA scale is the decisive missing experiment.

3.2 Reading the matrix: three result patterns and what each would mean

  • Pattern A — clean dissociation (desiderata hold at z, fail at h, yet h probes better): confirms the buffer account; the paper's contribution is the first quantitative transfer law and per-method transfer coefficients. Most likely outcome given RCDM/Guillotine. P
  • Pattern B — partial transfer tracks quality (methods whose h inherits more of the desideratum probe better within-family): would elevate transfer coefficients into a model-selection signal, extending RankMe beyond rank. O
  • Pattern C — the desiderata that matter at h are different ones (e.g., h-space effective rank and information retention predict downstream, while h-space alignment/uniformity don't): supports the sufficiency-not-minimality reading of §6 and reframes LeJEPA's contribution: its value would lie in choosing a distributional desideratum (isotropy) that transfers through the projector to h better than pointwise invariance, not in eliminating the projector. This is the most publishable outcome — it would say the field has been regularizing the wrong space with the right words. P

4 · Weaknesses of the SSL families

Failure modes sort into two kinds. Loss-blind semantic failures: the pretext is satisfied in z-space while the supervision signal it manufactures is wrong (crops of different objects declared "same"; backgrounds sufficing to solve instance discrimination). Objective-shape failures: the loss's implicit priors (uniform clusters, invariances, pixel-variance weighting, high-influence-feature bias) mismatch the data or task. Both kinds are invisible to loss curves and mostly invisible to IN-1k linear probes — hence the failure-oriented evaluations of §5.

4.1 View-based contrastive & distillation (SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL)

  • ECrop tension / object-centric dependence. Purushwalkam & Gupta (NeurIPS 2020): MoCo/PIRL "learn occlusion-invariant representations" but "fall short in capturing viewpoint and category instance invariance," and gains depend on "a clean object-centric training dataset like Imagenet." Mishra et al. (TMLR 2022) make it quantitative: random-resized-crop "performs poorly" on multi-small-object data; object-proposal crops give +8.8 mAP over scene-level cropping on OpenImages. Mo et al. (NeurIPS 2021): contrastive features are "contextually biased to the spurious scene correlations of different objects or object and background."
  • EBut contested: Van Gansbeke et al. (NeurIPS 2021) find contrastive methods effective across object- vs scene-centric and long-tailed data. PResolution: the failure is real for small-object/multi-object regimes and co-occurrence shortcuts, not a blanket scene-data failure — which is exactly why §5 probes it with controlled crop-content manipulations rather than dataset swaps alone.
  • EFeature suppression. Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2021): "a few bits of easy-to-learn shared features can suppress, and even fully prevent, the learning of other sets of competing features" (color suppresses class; "the dominant object would suppress the learning of smaller objects"). Robinson et al. (NeurIPS 2021): InfoNCE "does not always sufficiently guide which features are extracted," and fixing one suppression can create another. Xue et al. (ICML 2023) trace it to SGD simplicity bias. Li et al. (CVPR 2023) generalize: shortcuts come in multiples; "mitigating one amplifies others" (Whac-A-Mole; ImageNet-W watermark shortcut "affects nearly every modern vision model").
  • EBackground reliance & memorization. Backgrounds Challenge (Xiao et al., ICLR 2021): adversarial backgrounds fool models up to 87.5% (supervised baseline harness). Ryali et al.: background-augmented SSL gains +1–2 IN / up to +4.2 low-label across MoCo-v2/BYOL/SwAV — SSL demonstrably uses backgrounds. Extreme form — déjà vu memorization (Meehan et al., NeurIPS 2023): from a background-only crop of a training image, the foreground object is recoverable "with high accuracy," across SSL algorithms, and it "cannot be detected by conventional techniques for evaluating representation quality."
  • EInvariance mis-specification. LooC: color-invariant features fail color-sensitive tasks. InfoMin: optimal views are task-dependent. Ericsson et al.: "different downstream tasks benefit from polar opposite (in)variances"; SSL models "fail to preserve colour information as well as supervised alternatives." PPer §2, much augmentation information survives at h even when z is invariant — so downstream harm depends on the probe's access to h, another reason family-level claims need two-space measurement.
  • EShape/texture bias is not fixed by SSL. Geirhos et al. 2020: SimCLR-era SSL models are texture-biased with error patterns "surprisingly similar" to supervised; NeurIPS 2021 (85k psychophysical trials): contrastive-SSL and supervised models "systematically agree in their categorisation errors." ViT architecture, not SSL objective, moves shape bias (Naseer et al.). MIM/JEPA models are absent from this psychophysics literature. O
  • EAdversarial fragility with a z-space cause. Gupta et al. (AAAI 2023): contrastive SSL shows "intrinsically higher sensitivity to perturbations," attributed to hypersphere uniformity + false negatives — a stated-desideratum (uniformity) directly implicated in a robustness failure. PWhether the fragility lives in h or only in z-adjacent geometry is untested — audit cell.

4.2 Clustering & prototype methods (SwAV, DINO; DINOv2's data pipeline)

  • EThe hidden uniform cluster prior (Assran et al., ICLR 2023): SimCLR/VICReg/SwAV/MSN all "learn features that enable uniform clustering" — excellent on class-balanced ImageNet, and it "can hamper performance when pretraining on class-imbalanced data." A z-space equipartition constraint acting as a dataset-level assumption.
  • ECuration dependence. Tian et al. (ICCV 2021): large ImageNet-vs-YFCC quality gap from heavy-tailed class shift. DINOv2's answer is an automatic curation pipeline (LVD-142M) — Pengineering away the uncurated-data failure rather than fixing the objective. Counterpoints: Liu et al. (ICLR 2022) — SSL is more robust to imbalance than supervised; SEER — uncurated pretraining improves fairness/robustness metrics. The two claims are compatible (relative robustness vs absolute quality degradation) but their joint shape is unmeasured. O
  • EMulti-crop as a failure surface. DINO's local 96px crops (<50% area) train "local-to-global correspondences" — semantically, the model is required to agree about an image it mostly cannot see; on scene data local views may contain a different object than the global view. First dedicated task-conflict analysis only appeared Feb 2026 (De Plaen et al.: per-view-type predictors +3.8–4% — the shared head is forced to solve heterogeneous alignment tasks). Object-absent local views remain unstudied. O
  • OPrototype pathologies (dead/duplicated prototypes, long-tail prototype-class mismatch, what 131k prototypes encode at DINOv2 scale): no dedicated failure study found.

4.3 Masked image modeling (MAE, iBOT's MIM half)

  • ELinear-separability deficit, by the paper's own account: MAE linear probes (ViT-B 68.0 / L 75.8 / H 76.6) trail MoCo v3; "linear probing and fine-tuning results are largely uncorrelated"; one fine-tuned block: 73.5→81.0.
  • EPixel-variance shortcut, formalized: Balestriero & LeCun (ICML 2024): reconstruction "allocates a model's capacity towards a subspace of the data explaining the observed variance — a subspace with uninformative features"; on TinyImageNet the top-variance subspace (90% of pixel variance) supports only 45% accuracy vs 55% for the bottom 20%; perception features are "learned last," explaining MAE's long schedules; masking realigns the objective only partially.
  • EFrequency/attention signatures: Park et al. (ICLR 2023): MIM uses high-frequency (texture-adjacent) signals, contrastive uses low-frequency/global shapes; contrastive attention collapses into homogeneity late, MIM keeps locality and head diversity (Xie et al., CVPR 2023) — the two families are complementary, and their h-spaces should look measurably different (CKA cell in §3).
  • EThe best MIM features aren't at the top: MIM-Refiner (ICLR 2025): "strong representations within MIM models generally reside in intermediate layers" — a within-backbone guillotine effect: MAE's last blocks specialize toward reconstruction, i.e. the head boundary is fuzzy and partially inside the encoder. PAny h-space audit of MAE must therefore be layer-resolved, not last-layer-only.
  • EMasked-region ambiguity: masked prediction is ill-posed — "we can guess that there is a tail, but we cannot determine its exact location" (Bar et al., ICML 2024; stochastic positional embeddings +1.7 linear). But StoP addresses location ambiguity only; nobody quantifies content multimodality (how many completions a mask admits) or links that entropy to representation damage. O
  • EU-MAE: MAE features dimensionally collapse without an added uniformity term (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2022) — the anti-collapse desideratum, absent from MAE's design, turns out to bind at h.

4.4 Joint-embedding predictive (I-JEPA; LeJEPA)

  • EFragile anti-collapse: C-JEPA (NeurIPS 2024) identifies "the inefficacy of EMA from I-JEPA in preventing entire collapse" and patches it with VICReg terms. LeJEPA's abstract reads as the internal verdict: JEPA practice was "ad-hoc R&D" on a heuristic stack.
  • EContext-insufficiency has no relief valve: I-JEPA's own paper requires targets of "sufficiently large scale (semantic)" and "sufficiently informative" context; Littwin et al. (SSLTP 2024): the encoder "cannot adaptively modulate the type of predicted... features based on the feasibility of the masked prediction task" — when context underdetermines the target, the objective still demands a point estimate in latent space. PThis is MAE's ambiguity problem relocated to latent space, where averaging over modes produces blurred semantics instead of blurred pixels — plausibly worse and definitely less visible.
  • EImplicit bias toward high-influence features (Littwin et al., NeurIPS 2024): in deep linear models, JEPAs favor high-regression-coefficient features vs reconstruction's variance-weighting — the flip side is potential neglect of low-influence-but-task-relevant detail; V-JEPA 2.1 (2026) reports exactly that phenotype: dense features "do not emerge reliably when the prediction loss is applied only to masked regions"; DMT-JEPA: "insufficient understanding of local semantics."
  • EProbe dependence: V-JEPA: "no a priori reason for the encoder to yield a linearly separable subspace" — attentive-vs-linear gap of 16–17 points; I-JEPA takes best-of-two pooling schemes and evaluates the EMA teacher. Low-shot semantics: I-JEPA ViT-H 73.3 vs iBOT ViT-L 81.0 on IN-1% — augmentation-based methods still dominate low-shot by ~7–8 points.
  • ONo independent autopsy exists: every published I-JEPA critique is a method paper justifying its fix, several from affiliated labs. A neutral layer-wise, bias-profiled, two-space audit of I-JEPA has not been done — a concrete opening for this study.
  • PLeJEPA's exposure: LeJEPA keeps a 3-layer MLP projector and imposes SIGReg on its output, so — like every joint-embedding method — the projector can absorb the isotropy constraint while the backbone stays anisotropic. Its theorem proves isotropy is optimal for the probed distribution, but isotropy is enforced one space away from the probe. Whether SIGReg's distributional constraint transfers to h better than pointwise invariance does (and whether its heuristics-free training changes the transfer) is untested — and is the most interesting thing to measure about it. O

4.5 Cross-cutting evaluation gaps

  • EIN-1k linear accuracy correlates with many-shot transfer but "increasingly less" with few-shot, detection, dense prediction (Ericsson et al., CVPR 2021); single-metric evaluation can't say why a model is better (Dubois et al., ICML 2023); 700-experiment study questions the benchmarks outright (Kotar et al., ICCV 2021).
  • EProbe fragility (MAE linear↮finetune; V-JEPA attentive gap; Shi et al.: the probe layer "itself can be susceptible to spurious correlations") means family rankings are substantially protocol artifacts.
  • ONamed absences (all searched, none found): a group-robustness audit of SSL encoders (Waterbirds/CelebA worst-group); controlled IN-C/R/A comparison across families under matched backbone+probe; shape-bias psychophysics for MIM/JEPA; iBOT-specific failure analyses; SSL memorization beyond déjà vu. Protocol

6.1 Eight claims that weaken across the gap

  1. P"Good alignment + uniformity ⇒ good representation." Both metrics live on the loss sphere; Guillotine's core observation is that the layer where invariance is enforced is not the best layer; Xue et al. show h contains features z lacks entirely. Loss-sphere geometry under-determines h.
  2. PContrastive-loss-value generalization bounds. They bind g∘f; Saunshi et al. prove the loss value alone can be vacuous — the head is exactly the architectural degree of freedom their critique licenses.
  3. P"Learned features are the augmentation graph's spectral embedding." True of z at the optimum; h is a pre-image that need not carry the spectral solution's whitening/orthogonality structure — Guillotine's layer-wise probes show the trunk does not behave like the loss-optimal embedding.
  4. P"Invariance ⇒ minimal sufficient representation." By data processing, h is a superset of z's information — non-minimal by design (Ouyang: the head "filters out the information irrelevant to the contrastive objective"). Sufficiency-type conclusions survive at h; minimality/compression-type conclusions do not. And per Wang et al., that's fortunate.
  5. PIsotropy/whitening optimality prescriptions. Theorems of the form "the probed distribution should be isotropic/uniform" are enforced in a space nobody probes — including in LeJEPA, whose SIGReg targets isotropy at the projector output while the backbone is probed. So LeJEPA's isotropy-optimality theorem, taken literally, binds the discarded space; its applicability to the backbone requires the (unproven) assumption that isotropy transfers through the projector.
  6. PDimensional-collapse diagnoses at the embedding. Rank at z ≠ rank at h; the head buffers collapse pressure (Jing: no projector ⇒ collapse moves into h). Rank-based model selection (RankMe) rests on an empirical, not formal, h↔z monotonicity.
  7. PNon-contrastive stability theorems. DirectPred/IsoLoss/orthogonality results characterize predictor eigenmodes — the loss-adjacent machinery — while the object of interest is the encoder those dynamics shape (Wen & Li is the corrective, and its very existence marks the others' scope).
  8. PInformation-theoretic bounds on VICReg-style objectives. The certified quantities (entropy floor, decorrelation) are satisfied at z exactly and at h only directionally — the bound's premises fail in the probed space.

6.2 The results that survive — and their common shape

PFive kinds of result cross the gap: (i) theorems whose object is the encoder-under-a-head (Wen & Li); (ii) reductions that pull a far-space loss back to encoder-level structure (U-MAE); (iii) explicit backbone-side guarantees with the head modeled as a filter (Ouyang et al.); (iv) architectures that shrink the gap (DirectCLR — loss on a backbone sub-vector; note LeJEPA does not belong here, as it retains a full projector); (v) content claims — identifiability up to affine maps (LDM; Kong et al.) — which concern what is encoded rather than how it is arranged, and so are stable under the head's reparametrization. The pattern: geometry-claims break at the gap; content-claims and head-aware claims survive. That is also exactly the E1/E4 dichotomy — measure geometry desiderata (expect dissociation) and information content (expect survival) separately.

6.3 Where the two 2025–26 anchors sit

LeJEPA supplies a normative target: among distributions with a scalar covariance constraint, the isotropic Gaussian uniquely minimizes integrated squared bias of downstream probes — for linear and nonlinear probes — so this is the distribution to aim for. But LeJEPA enforces it on a projector, not the backbone, so the theorem currently certifies the wrong space; closing that gap (SIGReg directly on h) is an experiment, not an established property. Its plausible empirical bet — that a distributional constraint transfers through the projector more faithfully than pointwise invariance — is exactly what E10's SIGReg-on-backbone arm tests. P

Latent Distribution Matching (Mikulasch & Zenke, ICML 2026 Spotlight — verified) unifies the families at the loss layer: every surveyed objective is alignment (log-likelihood under an assumed latent model) plus an entropy estimator, and the choice of estimator generates the family tree — KDE→contrastive, parametric-Gaussian→VICReg, conditional-entropy-with-predictor→BYOL/SimSiam's stop-gradient, with CPC and JEPA also mapped. Its identifiability theorem (representations recover true latents up to affine transformation, even with nonlinear predictors) is the strongest available answer to "what do these objectives actually promise?" — and notably, the paper does not discuss projection heads at all. PComposing LDM's loss-layer identifiability with the head question is an open theory problem this study's data would directly inform: if z identifies latents up to affine maps and h = g−1-ish pre-image retains strictly more information, then h identifies them too, plus nuisances — formalizing "sufficiency survives, minimality doesn't." 7 · Synthesis, unified pattern & roadmap

7.1 Is there a unified pattern across SSL families?

EYes, at two levels. Empirically, every joint-embedding family enforces its desiderata in a discarded space and relies on the same buffer effect — and the loss space frequently fails to satisfy its own stated properties (contrastive embeddings dimensionally collapse; SimCLR's z is only "isotropic" in aspiration). Theoretically, the two 2025–26 anchors supply the unification the field lacked:

  • Latent Distribution Matching (Mikulasch & Zenke, ICML 2026 Spotlight — existence and venue verified, arXiv:2605.03517) casts all surveyed objectives as one template: alignment (log-likelihood under an assumed latent model) + uniformity (an entropy estimator), with the estimator choice generating the family tree (KDE → SimCLR; parametric Gaussian → VICReg; conditional-entropy-with-predictor → BYOL/SimSiam stop-gradient; CPC/JEPA also mapped). Its identifiability theorem — latents recovered up to an affine map — is the strongest statement of what these losses promise. It is stated at the loss layer and does not mention heads, which is precisely the seam this report probes.
  • LeJEPA (Balestriero & LeCun, arXiv:2511.08544; "L-JEPA" resolves here) supplies the normative counterpart: the isotropic Gaussian uniquely minimizes worst-case downstream risk. It removes the heuristic stack (stop-grad, EMA, teacher, schedulers) but — as its implementation confirms — still imposes that desideratum on a 3-layer MLP projector and probes the backbone. So it names the right target without yet enforcing it in the probed space.

PRead together: LDM says the families differ only in how they estimate the same two quantities in loss space; LeJEPA says pick one distributional desideratum (isotropy) and prove it optimal — yet still enforces it on a projector. The unified picture is that the field has spent five years regularizing the discarded space with increasingly sophisticated estimators, and not even the newest "provable" method has actually moved the desideratum onto the backbone. That move — SIGReg (or any desideratum) imposed directly on h — remains the untaken step this report's study is designed to test.

7.2 Do different methods satisfy different desiderata on the backbone?

PThe predicted answer (Table 3, §3.1) is that none of the classic methods satisfy their advertised desideratum at h — but they differ in which other properties survive:

  • Contrastive (SimCLR/MoCo): h inherits partial alignment and strong linear separability, but not invariance (RCDM), not uniformity, not decorrelation. Low-frequency/global structure; texture-biased.
  • Distillation (BYOL/DINO): h inherits high effective rank and excellent k-NN structure as an emergent, unstated property; the stated non-collapse desideratum is a head-dynamics fact, not an h-property.
  • Redundancy-reduction (BT/VICReg): h inherits a raised variance floor directionally (VICReg D.8) but the decorrelation itself is unmeasured at h — the family's central claim is untested in the probed space.
  • Clustering (SwAV/DINO): the one desideratum that does reach h is a negative one — the uniform-cluster prior, which manifests as a dataset assumption (imbalance sensitivity).
  • MIM (MAE): h uniquely retains low-level/positional and nuisance information (the opposite failure); its stated "sufficiency" is real but its linear separability is weak and best features sit mid-backbone.
  • JEPA (I-JEPA / LeJEPA): h inherits latent-predictability but with fragile anti-collapse and weak local semantics. LeJEPA is no exception to the gap — its isotropy target is enforced at the projector and merely hoped to transfer to the probed backbone; whether it does is the open question that would make or break its "provable" framing at the representation level.

7.3 Robust, fragile, underexplored

Most robust on the backbone
DINOv2 (KoLeo pushes a desideratum near h; best frozen features; curated-data-hardened) and iBOT (MIM+distillation hybrid, best low-shot) — though DINOv2's robustness is partly bought with data curation, and iBOT is nearly un-audited.
Most fragile / conditional
I-JEPA (fragile EMA anti-collapse, context-insufficiency, probe-sensitive, low-shot deficit) and MAE (weak linear probe, pixel-shortcut, needs fine-tuning) — strong only under their preferred readout.
Most likely to surprise
LeJEPA (its isotropy target is proven optimal but enforced at a projector — does it transfer to h?) and the redundancy-reduction pair (decorrelation at h has simply never been measured).
Most underexplored weaknesses
SSL group-robustness (no canonical audit), content-multimodality of masked prediction, prototype-head anatomy at scale, MIM/JEPA shape-bias psychophysics, iBOT failure modes.

7.5 What the independent adversarial verification established — and what it didn't

EA separate verification pass (6 search angles → 23 primary sources → 101 extracted claims → 25 ranked claims → 3-vote refutation panels each) returned 21 confirmed, 4 refuted. Confirmed 3-0: the loss-space/eval-space gap is real and foundational; no universal optimal readout layer; the head absorbs specialization while the backbone retains removed information; contrastive dimensional collapse in loss space; the prediction-head substitution/acceleration mechanism; DirectPred's analytic predictor; the projector as uniformity-projector (with entropy numbers); RankMe's cross-space predictivity; Wang & Isola characterizing only the normalized loss space. Confirmed at 2-1 (framing weighted lower): pre-projector versatility, predictor+stop-grad as the collapse-prevention pair (four factors interact, not two), and the object-centric-bias critique.

Refuted or contested — carried forward as caveats: (1) All three claims from arXiv:2605.17180 (linear-head implicit whitening; smooth-nonlinearity negative-curvature escape; "universal geometric buffer") failed verification (1-2, 1-2, 0-3) — this report does not rely on them. (2) The claim that CKA reliably identifies cross-model representation correspondence was refuted 0-3 — CKA is treated as contested in the §3 battery. Honest scope limit: the verification pass confirmed the projector-gap core to a high standard but reached no verified claims about MAE/I-JEPA/LeJEPA specifics, SwAV/clustering internals, the failure-oriented experiment designs, or the LDM paper — those rest on the four specialist dossiers (which verified them by direct quotation against arXiv) rather than on the adversarial panel. Treat the §1 method details, §4 MIM/JEPA failures, and §6 JEPA theory as quote-verified but not adversarially cross-examined. Post-review correction (2026-07-02): an earlier draft claimed LeJEPA applied its loss directly to the backbone (making it "1 of 11" without the gap). A reader checked the official implementation (galilai-group/lejepa) and found a 3-layer MLP projector with both losses computed on its output; the paper's ambiguous f_θ/z notation and its unexplained "proj. dim." hyperparameter had been misread. Corrected throughout: 0 of 11 methods impose their loss on the probed representation, and LeJEPA is the sharpest illustration of the gap rather than its exception. This is a cautionary example of the same failure the report studies — reading a paper's stated desideratum instead of checking where the code applies it.

7.6 Prioritized experimental roadmap for a first paper

  1. Phase 0 — instrument & pre-register (2 weeks). Fix the metric battery (§3, with CKA paired to a second geometry measure), the two-probe-per-space protocol, and the Table-3 directional predictions. Deliverable: a pre-registration that makes the central claim falsifiable before any number is computed.
  2. Phase 1 — the two-space audit on public checkpoints (E1, E2, E3; ~1 month, ~2–4 A100-weeks). This is the paper's spine and is publishable alone. Output: the completed metrics × models × spaces matrix, per-method transfer ratios τ, guillotine curves for desiderata (not just accuracy), and the regression of downstream performance on each metric×space. Decision gate: does h dissociate from z as predicted, and do h-space metrics out-predict z-space ones?
  3. Phase 2 — the augmentation-information ledger + two highest-value failure probes (E4, E5, E6; ~1 month). E4 tests the buffer theory directly and locates LeJEPA; E5 (object-absent/background crops via COCO masks) turns the crop-tension dispute into a graded per-family measurement; E6 delivers the missing SSL group-robustness audit. Each is a self-contained result.
  4. Phase 3 — the causal intervention grid (E10; ~4–6 A100-months, parallelizable). The move-the-loss retrains (VICReg-on-h, SimCLR projector-depth sweep, SIGReg-on-backbone LeJEPA, KoLeo-at-h) convert correlation into causation and yield the partial-transfer law — the strongest single contribution, and the natural second paper if compute is tight.
  5. Phase 4 — remaining failure probes + probe-sensitivity map (E7, E8, E9, E11; features already cached). Cheap given Phase 1 infrastructure; completes the shape-bias-for-MIM/JEPA and masked-ambiguity gaps and produces the probe-invariant reporting standard.

The minimum publishable unit is Phase 0 + Phase 1 + one failure probe — executable on public checkpoints in a small-lab budget, with a falsifiable central claim and a constructive deliverable (the four-class evaluation standard). The central claim to defend: SSL methods satisfy their advertised desiderata in the space they discard; what survives into the backbone is sufficiency-like, not the minimality/invariance/isotropy the objectives name — and measuring the right property in the right space explains downstream success better than the loss value does.
